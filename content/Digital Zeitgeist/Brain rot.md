---
title: Brain rot
---

**Brain rot** (also spelled **brainrot**) is a slang term expressing the sense that too much time spent on simple or repetitive media makes it harder to focus or think clearly. It is mostly used to talk about social media, notably short-form videos and online memes. It can also refer to the content itself, such as videos that are intentionally weird, repetitious, or nonsensical. The phrase "brain rot" is a common cultural expression, not the name of any recognized medical illness.<sup><a id="cite-ref-1-a" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-1">[1]</a></sup>

### History

The phrase "brain rot" went viral on the internet in the 2020s, but the meaning is far older. Oxford University Press says it was first used in print in 1854, in the book *Walden* by Henry David Thoreau. Thoreau used the phrase to critique a society that prefers simple thoughts to more sophisticated ones. He wasn't talking about digital media, but his point is not unlike today's worries that people may cease working their minds.<sup><a id="cite-ref-1-b" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-1">[1]</a></sup>

As online entertainment became a normal element of everyday life, the phrase developed a new meaning. As of 2024, brain rot frequency has climbed 230% compared to 2023, according to Oxford University Press. After examining language data and a public poll of more than 37,000 individuals, it chose brain rot as its 2024 Word of the Year. It was added to the Oxford English Dictionary officially in June 2025.<sup><a id="cite-ref-1-c" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-1">[1]</a><a id="cite-ref-2-a" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Use on social media

Brain rot is a term widely used on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube to describe jokes that are very repetitive, weird sound effects and short videos that have little discernible story. Some things are supposed to be silly, not educational. Folks may joke that after watching movies for hours on end that they have brain rot but this is usually in jest.

It is notably connected with Generation Z and Generation Alpha. Sometimes it is used to define a whole type of internet humor, instead of a poor emotional state. Memes popularized terms like "skibidi" and "only in Ohio", which were later [[Chopped|echoed in ordinary speech]]. Often, understanding these jokes requires knowing other web jokes. Brain rot language can therefore be a way for people to recognize each other in the same online group.<sup><a id="cite-ref-1-d" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Examples and related trends

A highly debated example is [*Skibidi Toilet*](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL-ZXraMeHBPJHXBhrNowJaQslyqtUg-tZ), an animated web video series depicting human-headed toilets and other strange characters. Its quick cuts, looping music and off-kilter humor have been called examples of "brain rot" entertainment.<sup><a id="cite-ref-1-e" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Another example is Italian brainrot, a viral fad that exploded around 2025. It has AI-generated characters that mix animals with common objects. These include a shark in sneakers and dancer Cappuccina, a dancer with a coffee cup for a head. Often, the characters are shown with exaggerated Italian-sounding narration and meaningless language. Videos are often duplicated, modified, and remixed, such that the gags circulate across other platforms.<sup><a id="cite-ref-3-a" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Public discussion

Sometimes the term is used seriously when individuals discuss screen time. You might label a long browse through TikTok "brain rot," even if you enjoyed the videos. The word could be used by parents and teachers in discussions about whether brief films are distracting young people from homework, reading or sleep. But brain rot is not a medical condition. There is no evidence that the brain literally rots from watching these videos, either.<sup><a id="cite-ref-4-a" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The word is not always used in a critical sense. It's also used to characterize jokes that people like, especially ones that look weird to anyone outside their online group. Sometimes the joy is to repeat the same sounds, words or letters. Researcher Emilie Owens has examined teenagers' use of "brain rot" on TikTok for online fun and to connect with others.<sup><a id="cite-ref-5-a" data-router-ignore href="#cite-note-5">[5]</a></sup>
